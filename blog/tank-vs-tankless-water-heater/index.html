Tank vs. tankless water heaters: real costs for Ohio homes

Tankless isn't always the upgrade it sounds like. Here's how the numbers really compare, from a plumber who installs both every week.

By Dana Whitfield, Master plumber
Updated November 3, 2026 · 6 min read

The short answerA tank water heater costs about half as much to install and is the better value for most households of one to three people. A tankless heater costs more up front but lasts around twice as long, never runs out of hot water and saves space, which suits larger families and homes staying in the family.

Side-by-side comparison

Gas tank (50 gal)Gas tankless
Installed pricefrom $1,795from $3,200
Typical lifespan8 – 12 yearsaround 20 years
Hot water supplyLimited by tank sizeContinuous

Running costs

A tank heater keeps 40 to 50 gallons hot around the clock, losing a little heat all day. A tankless unit only fires when you open a tap, so it skips that standby loss. For a typical family, that usually works out to a modest saving on gas each year: enough to matter over 20 years, but rarely enough to pay back the higher price quickly on its own.

The winter detail most buyers miss

In Ohio, the water coming into your house is much colder in January than in July. A tankless heater has to raise that cold water to shower temperature instantly, so its flow rate drops in winter. We size every tankless unit for the coldest incoming water, so two showers can run at once in February, not just in summer.

What the install really involves

  • A larger gas line, because tankless units burn more gas while running
  • New venting, usually PVC through a side wall
  • An electrical outlet nearby for the controls
  • Isolation valves so the unit can be descaled each year

Which one I'd choose

For a couple or small family in a starter home, I'd install a good tank heater and put the savings elsewhere. For a family of four or more, a home with a large soaking tub, or a house you plan to stay in for 15 years or longer, tankless is worth it.

Whatever you pick, flush or descale it once a year. It's the cheapest way to make a water heater last.

Quick questions

Does a tankless water heater work during a power outage?

No. Gas tankless units need electricity for their controls and fan, so they stop during an outage. Many gas tank heaters with a standing pilot keep working.

Can I replace my tank with tankless in one day?

Usually, yes. Most conversions are finished in one long day, including the gas line and venting changes.
